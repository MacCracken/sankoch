# 2.7.0 — xz encoder speedup baseline (before the match-finder / optimal-parse work)

**Date**: 2026-07-20 · **Machine**: x86_64 linux · **Toolchain**: Cyrius 6.4.68 ·
**Commit**: `3e3ce4e` (2.6.4) · **Reference**: xz-utils 5.8.3 · **Prior baseline**:
[`2026-07-19-2.5.9-p1-baseline.md`](2026-07-19-2.5.9-p1-baseline.md)

The 2.7.0 arc targets the single largest measured performance gap in the tree: `.xz`
**encode** throughput. This doc is the "before" — a measured baseline plus a profiling
attribution that determines *what to fix*. Method: a scratch harness reads each fixture into
memory and times `xz_compress` (best-of-3, monotonic `clock_gettime`); the reference column is
`xz -6`/`-9` timed the same way (best-of-3, subprocess — the synthetic-fixture reference MB/s is
floored by ~5 ms of process-spawn overhead, so treat those as *lower bounds on the gap*).

xz encode is **not** in the wire-format SIZE gate — it ships informational ratio lines — so the
2.7.0 work may change the encoder's exact output bytes, subject to two hard constraints:
**ratio equal-or-better** and **every stream still decodes via `xz -d`**.

## Throughput + ratio baseline

| fixture | input | sankoch out | sankoch MB/s | `xz -6` out | `xz -6` MB/s | speed gap | ratio gap |
|---------|------:|------------:|-------------:|------------:|-------------:|----------:|-----------|
| corpus (real Cyrius source) | 321,871 | 74,132 | **0.59** | 68,192 | 4.4 | **~7.3×** | +8.7 % larger |
| text 256K (45-byte pattern) | 262,144 | 240 | **0.15** | 224 | ~50 | **~300×** | +7 % larger |
| zeros 256K | 262,144 | 192 | **0.07** | 180 | ~55 | **~750×** | +7 % larger |

The gap is ~7× on real data but explodes to 300–750× on repetitive data. The corpus ratio gap
(+8.7 %) is mostly the **32 KB match window** (`LZ77_WINDOW`) vs `xz -6`'s 8 MB dictionary —
long-range matches in a 314 KB file are simply unreachable.

## Profiling attribution (the finding that sets the plan)

Counter instrumentation (reverted after measurement), per single encode. `mf` = HC3 match
finder (`_xze_get_matches` chain walk + byte extension); `dp` = optimal-parse relaxations
(`_xze_dp_fill` per-length pricing); `rep` = the 4-rep byte-extension loop.

| fixture | mf chain-nodes | mf extend-bytes | dp relaxations | rep extend-bytes | dominant cost |
|---------|---------------:|----------------:|---------------:|-----------------:|---------------|
| corpus  | **23.8M** | 11.5M | 6.8M | 3.0M | **match finder (~78 %)** |
| text 256K | 0.27M | 69M | **137M** | 69M | **DP + rep (~75 %)** |
| zeros 256K | 0.26M | 69M | **344M** | **277M** | **DP + rep (~90 %)** |

**Conclusion.** Two different bottlenecks in two different regimes:

1. **Real data** → the HC3 hash-chain walk dominates (23.8M chain nodes on 314 KB). This is the
   classic match-finder gap the roadmap named; a **BT4/HC4** finder (4-byte hash, `nice_len`
   early-out, self-pruning tree) is the fix.
2. **Repetitive data** → the match finder is *negligible* (~0.26M nodes); the cost is the
   optimal parse **pricing a ~273-length match at every one of the 4096 window positions**, plus
   **extending all 4 reps 273 bytes at every position**. The reference LZMA parse avoids this
   with a **`nice_len` greedy shortcut** — take a long match immediately, skip the DP over the
   covered positions. This is the larger prize and the roadmap did *not* name it.

So the 2.7.0 order is **optimal-parse `nice_len` shortcut first** (attacks the 300–750×
repetitive gap), **BT4/HC4 match finder second** (attacks the ~7× real-data gap), tuning third —
the reverse of the roadmap's "match finder → parse → tuning", justified by the numbers above.

## Results — 2.7.0 (Bites 1+2: `nice_len` greedy shortcut + interior cut)

2.7.0 ships the optimal-parse half of the plan: a **rep-only `nice_len` greedy shortcut** at
the window start (Bite 1) and an **interior cut** when a rep covers the whole remaining DP
window (Bite 2). Both are *rep-only* and gated on a **saturated** rep (the full match length /
the full window) — the ratio-safe subset the adversarial design review isolated (a mid-length
greedy match can lose to the windowed DP, so only saturated reps, which carry zero distance
cost and maximal coverage, are taken greedily). The HC4 match finder (the real-data half) is
deferred to 2.7.1.

| fixture | baseline MB/s | 2.7.0 MB/s | speedup | baseline out | 2.7.0 out | ratio |
|---------|--------------:|-----------:|--------:|-------------:|----------:|-------|
| text 256K | 0.15 | **44.6** | **~290×** | 240 | **216** | −10 % (better) |
| zeros 256K | 0.07 | **31.7** | **~473×** | 192 | **172** | −10 % (better) |
| corpus (real source) | 0.59 | 0.59 | 1.0× | 74,132 | 74,132 | exact (neutral) |

The repetitive-data gap (the roadmap's headline ~400–900×) is essentially closed — text/zeros
are now within ~1.5× of reference `xz -6`, **and smaller** (the greedy saturated-rep parse beats
the old windowed DP on these). Corpus is exactly neutral: the greedy shortcut fires only on
saturated reps, which never occur suboptimally on real source (a full `XZE_NICE`-value sweep
64→192 all lost 4 bytes on corpus; only the saturated-rep trigger = 273 held byte-exact). All
three streams still decode via `xz -t` / `xz -dc | cmp` (xz-utils 5.8.3), and the 43 gated
`bench` SIZE lines are byte-identical (xz encode is informational, not gated).

**Still open — 2.7.1:** corpus is unchanged (0.59 vs `xz -6` 4.4 MB/s = 7.3×). Its cost is 78 %
HC3 hash-chain walk; the HC4 finder (4-byte hash + 3-byte seed + `nice_len` early-out on
xz-private tables) is the 2.7.1 fix.
