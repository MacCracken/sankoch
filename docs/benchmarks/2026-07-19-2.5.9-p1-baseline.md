| **xz** | **24,458,983** | **55,404,026** | 1,916,772 |

**xz encode is ~130× slower than bzip2 and ~165× slower than zstd on the same 4 KB of
text**, and it is *slower on compressible input than on random input* — the optimal parse
does most work where there is most redundancy.

### xz encode scaling — measured, not extrapolated

Scaling is **linear**, so there is no algorithmic blowup; the constant factor is simply
very large. Directly measured (not extrapolated):

| input | size | sankoch `xz_compress` | out | throughput |
|-------|-----:|----------------------:|----:|-----------:|
| zeros | 4 KB | 59 ms | 92 | 0.07 MB/s |
| zeros | 64 KB | 902 ms | 144 | 0.07 MB/s |
| zeros | **1 MB** | **14,818 ms** | 384 | **0.068 MB/s** |
| text | 4 KB | 24 ms | 116 | 0.16 MB/s |
| text | 64 KB | 403 ms | 172 | 0.16 MB/s |
| text | **1 MB** | **6,435 ms** | 412 | **0.155 MB/s** |

Reference `xz -6`, same machine, 4 MB input (large enough that process startup is noise):
**62 ms → 64.5 MB/s**, i.e. ~16 ms for 1 MB.

| | sankoch | reference `xz -6` | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 MB zeros | 14,818 ms | ~16 ms | **~900×** |
| 1 MB text | 6,435 ms | ~16 ms | **~400×** |

**The ratio is not the problem — it is comparable or better.** sankoch produces 384 B for
1 MB of zeros; `xz -6` produces 752 B for 4 MB of zeros. sankoch pays roughly 900× the
time for equal-or-better output.

This is after the 2.5.4 encoder-throughput pass, which took xz optimal-parse ~5× faster on
text. The remaining gap is structural, not a missing micro-optimisation: reference xz uses
a BT4 binary-tree match finder with hash-chain skipping, while sankoch walks a plain chain
and prices every position.

**Practical impact**: `takumi` is listed as an xz-encode consumer. A 4 MB source tarball
would take ~25–57 s to compress. Recorded here as the largest measured performance gap in
the tree; disposition is in the P(-1) audit record.
