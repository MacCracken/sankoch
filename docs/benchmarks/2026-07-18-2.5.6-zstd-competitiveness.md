# 2.5.6 — zstd encoder competitiveness: ratios + level knob

**Date**: 2026-07-18 · **Machine**: x86_64 linux · **Toolchain**: Cyrius 6.4.67 ·
**Reference**: `zstd` CLI v1.5.7 (Yann Collet)

The 2.5.6 arc closed the encoder-competitiveness gap opened at 2.5.5: **FSE-compressed
literal weights** (wide/UTF-8/binary literals now Huffman-compress), **repeat-offset
codes**, a **one-step lazy parse**, and a **1..9 compression-level knob** (default 6).
**Every output below was decoded byte-identical by reference `zstd -d`** — the
correctness bar. Sizes are bytes; gap is sankoch vs `zstd -1` (the greedy level it
competes with).

|-------|-----:|-------------:|--------:|--------:|----------:|

## What moved since 2.5.5

At 2.5.5 the encoder was **+2 % to +17 % behind `zstd -1`**. At 2.5.6 it **beats `zstd -1`
on source, binary, repetitive, and incompressible data**, and trails only on UTF-8-heavy
text (+7.5 %, down from +17 %). Where each item helped:

- **FSE literal weights** — the biggest single win on wide-alphabet data: binaries and
  UTF-8 text previously stored literals *raw*; e.g. `deflate.cyr` went from +7 % to
  parity-ish before the parse work even landed.
- **Repeat-offset codes** — near-free `Offset_Value 1` (offset code 0) for recurring
  offsets; drives the repetitive/binary wins and never enlarges a stream.
- **Lazy parse** — flips source/binary from *behind* to *ahead* of `zstd -1`
  (`deflate.cyr` −2.3 %, `zstd.cyr` −0.9 %, `bash` −0.5 %).

The remaining +7.5 % on UTF-8 text is parse quality — zstd -1's repcode-aware match
finder vs our opportunistic repeat coding. **Repcode-aware matching is deferred to
2.5.7** (see roadmap); it also targets the tabular/record data where a greedy+lazy
finder still trails (a synthetic 132 K record set: ~+100 % vs -1).

## Compression-level knob (`zstd_compress_level`, 1..9)

Level maps to hash-chain search depth (8→512) + lazy on/off, mirroring the DEFLATE
convention; ratio is monotonic in the level. On `src/zstd.cyr` (76,198 B):

| level | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 (default) | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|------:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|:-----------:|--:|--:|--:|
| bytes | 26,568 | 24,550 | 24,210 | 24,036 | 23,959 | 23,908 | 23,861 | 23,834 | 23,803 |

Level 1 is greedy/shallow (fastest); level 9 searches deepest. The default (6) matches
the pre-knob behavior. Encode throughput ranges from ~fast (level 1, greedy) to ~8 MiB/s
worst-case (level 6+ on low-redundancy input — lazy roughly doubles the match search).

## Notes

- **No wire-format SIZE gate for zstd** — encoder output isn't bit-reproducible across
  encoder versions, so the `SIZE zstd6_*` lines in `cyrius bench` are informational
  ratio lines, not a drift gate (like the xz/bzip2 encoder lines).
- **Decoder hardening** (same arc) added no measurable decode-path cost — the guards are
  single compares on already-computed sizes; reference `zstd -19` output still decodes
  byte-identically (verified up to a 1.2 MB binary).
